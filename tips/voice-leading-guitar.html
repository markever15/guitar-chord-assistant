13. Voice Leading: Making Chord Changes Sound Smoother

Voice leading is choosing which version of a chord to play so the individual notes move as little as possible between changes. Two players can play the same progression from the same chart and sound completely different, and this is usually why.

What it sounds like

Play C then F using the open shapes everyone learns first. Every note jumps. It works, but each chord arrives as a separate event.

Now play C as x 3 2 0 1 0 and F as x x 3 2 1 1. The B and E on the top two strings barely move, and the change stops sounding like two unrelated shapes. Nothing about the harmony changed — only which notes you chose to play.

The rules, such as they are

  • Keep common tones. If two chords share a note, play that note in the same place in both. C and Am share C and E; a voicing that holds them makes the change nearly disappear.
  • Move the rest by the shortest distance. Where a note has to change, prefer a fret or two over an octave leap.
  • Watch the top note. The highest note is what listeners follow, so its path through a progression matters more than anything happening in the middle.
  • Watch the bass separately. The lowest note is a melody too, and it is the one slash chords exist to control.

Slash chords are the obvious tool

C–Am–F has bass notes C, A, F — all leaps. Rewrite it C–G/B–Am and the bass walks C–B–A. Extend it and you get C–G/B–Am–Am/G–F, with the bass stepping C–B–A–G–F under chords that barely move.

This is the single most reused voice-leading device in popular music, and on guitar it usually costs one finger.

Staying in one position

The other approach is to stop moving your hand at all. Instead of playing every chord in its open shape, find a voicing of each within four frets of the last one. Your fingers make small adjustments rather than resetting between chords, and the progression gains a continuity that open shapes cannot produce.

This is what the Chord Dictionary's position bands are for. Each chord is shown grouped by where on the neck it lives, so you can find, say, every version of F between the 5th and 8th frets and pick the one nearest to the C you just played.

When to ignore all of this

Smooth is not always right. A big jump between chords is a real effect, and songs use it deliberately when a section needs to open up. Voice leading is a tool for making a progression feel connected — sometimes you want the opposite.

It also matters less in a dense arrangement. If a band is playing, most of these details are buried. Voice leading earns its keep in exposed playing: solo guitar, a duo, fingerstyle, anything where the guitar is carrying the harmony on its own.

An exercise

Take C–Am–F–G. Play it with open shapes and listen. Then play it again with every chord voiced between the 5th and 8th frets, choosing whichever version of each is closest to the last. It will feel unfamiliar and sound noticeably more joined-up. Do that with three progressions you already know and you will stop reaching automatically for the first shape you learned — which is the whole point.

The top note is the melody

Of everything in a chord, listeners follow the highest note most closely. That makes it worth deciding rather than accepting.

Try playing a progression three times, choosing voicings so the top note stays on the same pitch throughout. Then again so it descends step by step. Then again so it climbs. The chords are identical each time and the three versions do not sound remotely alike — because you have effectively written three different melodies over the same harmony.

This is how chord-melody arranging works, and it is also why the same four chords can support completely different guitar parts on two different records.

Where to find these voicings

The Chord Dictionary shows a handful of representative shapes for each chord first, spread across the neck, with the full list one click away. When you are voice leading, the representatives are usually enough: pick the one nearest the fret you are already on, check whether it shares a note with the previous chord, and try it. The full list is there for the times when nothing in the shortlist fits under your hand.
